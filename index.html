Australia's Healthcare Workforce

Growing, but Where?

How has Australia's registered healthcare workforce changed, and where are its practitioners located?

01. A Workforce on the Rise

Australia's registered healthcare workforce has expanded, but growth has not been equal across professions.

National Workforce Growth

+17.9% growth in registered healthcare practitioners between 2020 and 2024

Australia's registered healthcare workforce grew from 642,479 practitioners in 2020 to 757,517 in 2024. National growth alone does not show how evenly practitioners are distributed across professions and regions.

02. Who Makes Up the Workforce?

Explore workforce composition and age across professions.

Workforce Composition

How to read: Each rectangle represents a healthcare profession. Larger rectangles indicate more registered practitioners. Colours distinguish profession groups, while the numbers indicate each profession's ranking by headcount. Hover over a rectangle to see its full name, practitioner count and share of the workforce.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

In 2024, nurses and midwives made up the largest share of Australia's registered healthcare workforce at 54.1%, followed by medical practitioners at 16.1%. Other professions, including physiotherapists, psychologists and pharmacists, accounted for much smaller proportions. This highlights how heavily the healthcare workforce relies on nursing and midwifery, while also showing the diversity of professions contributing to healthcare services.

Average Age by Profession

How to read: Each dot represents the average age of practitioners in a healthcare profession. The dashed orange line shows the overall workforce average of 42.1 years. Hover over a dot for more details.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

Australia's healthcare professions show noticeable differences in average practitioner age. Occupational therapists have the youngest workforce, averaging 36.7 years, while Chinese medicine practitioners have the oldest at 52.0 years. Compared with the overall average of 42.1 years, these differences highlight how the age profile of Australia's healthcare workforce varies across professions.

03. Where Are the Workers?

Compare workforce distribution across Australia's states and territories.

Healthcare Workforce Density by State

How to read: Darker teal indicates more registered healthcare practitioners per 100,000 residents. Hover over each state or territory to see its workforce density, total number of practitioners and population.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024); Australian Bureau of Statistics (2024).

Healthcare workforce density varies across Australia. South Australia has the highest number of registered practitioners relative to its population, with approximately 3,145 per 100,000 residents, followed by Tasmania at 3,019. New South Wales has the lowest density at approximately 2,523. These differences show that the distribution of healthcare professionals is not proportional to population size across states and territories.

Healthcare Workforce Headcount by State

How to read: Each circle represents the number of registered healthcare practitioners in a state or territory. Larger circles indicate larger workforces. Hover over a circle to see the exact headcount and its share of Australia's workforce.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

New South Wales has Australia's largest registered healthcare workforce, followed by Victoria and Queensland. Although South Australia has the highest workforce density relative to population, its total workforce is considerably smaller. Comparing these two maps highlights the difference between the overall number of healthcare workers and their availability relative to population size.

04. Beyond the Major Cities

Explore healthcare access and workforce patterns beyond metropolitan areas.

Australia's Public Hospital Locations

How to read: Each teal dot represents a public hospital location. Hover over a dot to see the hospital's name, suburb, state and type. Use the slider to adjust the size of the dots.

Source: Australian public hospital locations dataset (2026).

Australia's public hospitals are concentrated along the eastern and southeastern coast, particularly around major population centres. Hospitals are more widely dispersed across regional and remote areas, reflecting the challenges of providing healthcare services across Australia's large geographical area. However, hospital locations alone do not indicate the capacity or accessibility of healthcare services.

Healthcare Workforce Density by Remoteness

How to read: Each bar represents the number of registered healthcare practitioners per 100,000 residents in a remoteness category. Use the dropdown to switch between practitioner headcount and full-time equivalent (FTE) rates. Hover over the bars for exact figures.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

Healthcare workforce density generally decreases as areas become more remote. Major Cities have approximately 2,885 registered practitioners per 100,000 residents, compared with 1,994 in Very Remote areas. However, when measured using full-time equivalent (FTE), the difference becomes smaller, suggesting that differences in working hours partly offset lower practitioner numbers in remote communities.

Working Hours by Profession and Remoteness

How to read: Each cell shows the average weekly working hours for a healthcare profession in a remoteness category. Darker teal indicates longer working hours. Use the dropdown to highlight a profession, and hover over cells for exact figures.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

Healthcare workers in remote Australia generally work longer hours than those in major cities. Across all registered professions, average weekly hours increase from 35.5 in Major Cities to 43.3 in Very Remote areas. Paramedicine practitioners consistently work some of the longest hours, particularly in remote locations. These patterns help explain why the differences in full-time equivalent workforce density are smaller than the differences in practitioner headcount shown in Chart 10.

05. The Bigger Picture

Headcounts do not tell the whole story about available workforce capacity.

Headcount vs Full-Time Equivalent Workforce

How to read: Each pair of dots compares registered practitioner headcount (teal) with full-time equivalent workforce (orange). The connecting line shows the difference between the two. Use the View dropdown to explore smaller professions and the Highlight dropdown to focus on a particular profession. Hover over the dots for exact figures.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

The number of registered healthcare practitioners does not always reflect the equivalent full-time workforce available. Nurses and midwives have fewer FTE than registered practitioners, indicating that their combined working hours represent a smaller full-time equivalent workforce. In contrast, paramedicine practitioners have more FTE than their headcount, suggesting longer average working hours. These differences highlight why both headcount and FTE are important when assessing Australia's healthcare workforce capacity.

Key Takeaways: A Growing Workforce, Unevenly Distributed

Australia's registered healthcare workforce has grown considerably, increasing from 642,479 practitioners in 2020 to 757,517 in 2024, a rise of 17.9%. However, this growth does not mean that healthcare workers are equally available across the country.

01. A Growing but Uneven Workforce

Australia's healthcare workforce continues to expand, but growth varies between professions. Nurses and midwives remain the largest professional group, accounting for more than half of registered practitioners in 2024.

02. Geography Shapes Healthcare Availability

Healthcare workforce density differs across states and territories, with particularly noticeable differences between major cities and remote communities. The distribution of public hospitals also highlights the concentration of healthcare infrastructure along Australia's more populated coastal regions.

03. Remote Healthcare Workers Carry a Greater Workload

Healthcare practitioners in Very Remote Australia work an average of 43.3 hours per week, compared with 35.5 hours in Major Cities. This suggests that workers in remote communities may face greater workload demands.

04. Headcount Does Not Tell the Whole Story

The number of registered practitioners does not always reflect the equivalent full-time workforce. Differences between headcount and FTE show why working hours must also be considered when assessing healthcare workforce capacity.

The bigger picture: Australia's healthcare workforce is growing, but ensuring healthcare availability requires more than increasing practitioner numbers. Where healthcare workers are located, the professions they represent and the hours they work are equally important in understanding workforce capacity across Australia.
