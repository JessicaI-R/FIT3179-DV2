Australia's Healthcare Workforce

Growing, but Where?

How has Australia's registered healthcare workforce changed, and where are its practitioners located?

01. A Workforce on the Rise

Australia's registered healthcare workforce has expanded, but growth has not been equal across professions.

1. National Workforce Growth

+17.9% growth in registered healthcare practitioners between 2020 and 2024

Australia's registered healthcare workforce grew from 642,479 practitioners in 2020 to 757,517 in 2024. National growth alone does not show how evenly practitioners are distributed across professions and regions.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data, 2020–2024. The vertical axis does not begin at zero.

3. Fastest-Growing Professions

Occupational Therapists experienced the fastest percentage growth (34.2%), followed by Physiotherapists (26.8%) and Paramedicine Practitioners (24.9%).

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data, 2020 and 2024. Growth calculated from headcounts.

13. Changes in Profession Rankings

How to read: Rank 1 represents the largest profession by headcount. Orange lines indicate professions whose rankings changed at least once between 2020 and 2024. Hover over a line to highlight that profession.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data, 2020–2024.

Despite continued growth in Australia's registered healthcare workforce, the relative rankings of most professions remained stable between 2020 and 2024. Only a small number of professions changed positions, suggesting that workforce expansion has largely preserved the existing distribution of practitioners across professions.

02. Who Makes Up the Workforce?

Explore workforce composition and age across professions.

4. Workforce Composition

How to read: Each rectangle represents a healthcare profession. Larger rectangles indicate more registered practitioners. Colours distinguish profession groups, while the numbers indicate each profession's ranking by headcount. Hover over a rectangle to see its full name, practitioner count and share of the workforce.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

In 2024, nurses and midwives made up the largest share of Australia's registered healthcare workforce at 54.1%, followed by medical practitioners at 16.1%. Other professions, including physiotherapists, psychologists and pharmacists, accounted for much smaller proportions. This highlights how heavily the healthcare workforce relies on nursing and midwifery, while also showing the diversity of professions contributing to healthcare services.

5. Average Age by Profession

How to read: Each dot represents the average age of practitioners in a healthcare profession. The dashed orange line shows the overall workforce average of 42.1 years. Hover over a dot for more details.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024).

Australia's healthcare professions show noticeable differences in average practitioner age. Occupational therapists have the youngest workforce, averaging 36.7 years, while Chinese medicine practitioners have the oldest at 52.0 years. Compared with the overall average of 42.1 years, these differences highlight how the age profile of Australia's healthcare workforce varies across professions.

03. Where Are the Workers?

Compare workforce distribution across Australia's states and territories.

6. Healthcare Workforce Density by State

How to read: Darker teal indicates more registered healthcare practitioners per 100,000 residents. Hover over each state or territory to see its workforce density, total number of practitioners and population.

Source: Australian Government, Health Workforce Data (2024); Australian Bureau of Statistics (2024).

Healthcare workforce density varies across Australia. South Australia has the highest number of registered practitioners relative to its population, with approximately 3,145 per 100,000 residents, followed by Tasmania at 3,019. New South Wales has the lowest density at approximately 2,523. These differences show that the distribution of healthcare professionals is not proportional to population size across states and territories.
